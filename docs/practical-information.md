## Evaluation criteria

The organizers group judging into four areas.

| Criterion | Questions on the slide | Implication for DTOR / Copernicus project |
|---|---|---|
| **Impact & challenge relevance** | How effectively does the solution address a specific challenge and the needs of target users? Does it contribute meaningfully to the objectives of the European Ocean Pact? | Lead with a concrete user: deployment planners, ocean-observing-system researchers, or teams interpreting robot/Argo data. State the decision the tool improves. |
| **Innovativeness** | Is the approach novel or improved? Does it have clear advantages versus existing solutions? | Emphasize the combination of **Copernicus Marine fields + real robot observations + virtual observing-system simulations** in a reproducible Julia workflow. |
| **Progress** | How significant was progress during the event? Is there a credible pathway toward further development, deployment, or adoption? | Show visible development from a basic GLORYS12 map to an end-to-end regional demonstrator, and describe how it could become a reusable DTOR workflow. |
| **Technical capabilities** | Is there a clear demo? Does the solution effectively leverage Copernicus Marine data and services? Is it technically and practically feasible? | A working, robust, compact demo matters more than a broad architecture. Make GLORYS12 data visibly central—not merely background input. |
